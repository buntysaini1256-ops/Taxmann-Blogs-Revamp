In brief

Section 194J of the Income-tax Act, 1961, on fees for professional or technical services, has no section of its own in the Income-tax Act, 2025. It now sits as section 393(1), the table for payments to residents, read with the definitions in section 402. Much of the 1961 Act moved to new addresses like this, while the rulings you rely on still cite the old numbers. This post sets out where 194J sits, how to find the new section for any 1961 one, and how to cite older rulings.

1. Where section 194J sits now

The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, and earlier years still bring up the Income-tax Act, 1961. Section numbers have changed, and older rulings cite the old sections.

Section 194J shows how far the numbers moved. The 194 series no longer exists as separate sections: the deduction on fees for professional or technical services is now a row in the table in section 393(1), and the terms in it, such as “professional services” and “fees for technical services”, are defined in section 402. One 1961 section now sits across two 2025 sections.

For tax year 2025-26 and earlier years, though, the old number is still the one to use: CBDT’s FAQs on section 536 keep proceedings for those years under the 1961 Act, and tax year 2026-27 onwards falls under the 2025 Act. Our analysis of the Income-tax Act 2025 vs 1961 sets out which Act applies to which year.

2. How to find the new section for any 1961 section

Much of the 1961 Act carried over to new numbers, and one old section can sit across more than one new one, as 194J does. A reliable way to work:

  • Start from the section you know, and find its counterpart in the 2025 Act.
  • Read the counterpart sub-section by sub-section, because a change of words can change the answer.
  • Check the matching Rule too, in the Rules of 1962 and 2026.
  • Check the CBDT circulars and notifications on the provision, and which Act each one relates to.

3. Citing rulings that name the old sections

Most rulings you will cite were decided under the 1961 Act and use its section numbers. Before you cite one in a matter under the 2025 Act, compare the provision it interpreted with its 2025 counterpart. Where the wording carries over, the ruling is a strong starting point; where it changed, read the ruling with care.

Then check the ruling’s own history: whether later courts followed it, distinguished it, or reversed or overruled it. A quick check before you rely on a section:

  • Which Act governs the proceeding, and for which tax year?
  • Which section of that Act, and what is its counterpart in the other?
  • Do the rulings you cite interpret the same words?
  • Which circulars and notifications apply, and under which Act?

5. Frequently asked questions

Where is section 194J in the Income-tax Act, 2025?

In the table in section 393(1), for payments to residents, read with the definitions in section 402. Comparative (Act) in Taxmann.com | Research maps section 194J of the 1961 Act to sections 393 and 402 of the 2025 Act.

How do I find the new section for a 1961 section?

Start from the section you know, find its counterpart in the 2025 Act and read it sub-section by sub-section, with the matching Rule and circulars. Comparative (Act) in Taxmann.com | Research sets each section beside its counterpart.

Can I still cite rulings decided under the 1961 Act?

Yes, as a starting point where the 2025 provision carries the same words. Compare the provision the ruling interpreted with its counterpart first, and check how later courts treated the ruling.
